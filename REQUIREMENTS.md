# Agregador: Requirements

A Spanish-language news aggregator. It collects articles from the RSS feeds of popular media, groups articles about the same story into clusters, and publishes a front page with the ten most covered stories of each country. **Every text shown is a real headline written by an outlet, attributed and linked; no text is generated by AI.** The only machine learning left is a local embedding model used to cluster.

## 1. Scope

- **Editions**: two tabs, **International** (first; foreign outlets only, in English, no translation, 12 outlets spread over regions: US, UK, France, EU, Qatar, Israel, Hong Kong, India, Singapore, South Africa) and **Spain** (Spanish outlets, in Spanish). Each edition is clustered on its own; the page language (labels and dates) follows the edition, headlines are always shown as published. The site title and tab names are in English. Argentina is supported by the code but disabled in `feeds.toml`. Cross-language clustering is out of scope (tested on 2026-10-19: correct cross-lingual matches score 0.88-0.91 vs false ones 0.86-0.87, too close for a fixed threshold).
- **Daily editions** (FR-15): one edition per local day (Europe/Madrid), published at 07:00, covering the previous 24 h. It is a frozen snapshot of every tab: `<site>/<YYYY-MM-DD>/edition.json` (written once, never regenerated; `--force` replaces it) plus `img/` and the pages. The pages are a pure function of `edition.json`, so `agregador site` rebuilds all of them (new template, new archive neighbours) without touching the data. The home page is the latest edition; `<site>/archive/` lists every day by month; each edition links to the previous and next one. Collection (`fetch`, every hour at minute 51, light: feeds keep only a few hours) is separate from the daily work (`run` = fetch, purge, embed, cluster, then `edition`, at 07:00); `render` is only a live preview without images.
- **Story images** (FR-16): the top stories that show a picture (positions 1, 2, 4, 5, 6) get a thumbnail saved with the edition (WebP, at most 800 px wide, about 30 KB; about 0.6 MB per edition, 220 MB a year). The first usable image among the story's articles is taken, re-encoded (the original's metadata is dropped), and credited to the outlet it came from. Each image stays tied to its story: the file name is `<tab>-<rank>-<cluster id>.webp`, its EXIF carries the headline and the source article and image URLs, and `edition.json` records for each story its image (file, size, sha256, original URL, outlet, article URL) next to every article of the story. Images are fetched only from public hosts (no loopback/LAN), at most 8 MB, 40 MP, no more than 3 redirects. **Open legal point**: serving copies of third-party images is a reproduction, not a link; get advice before the site goes public.
- **Content**: RSS data only (title, summary, link, publication date, image if present). No full-article fetching in v1.
- **Audience**: personal use first, public product later. Design choices must not block going public.
- **Hosting**: one Linux box on Hetzner (e.g. 2 vCPU / 4 GB). No GPU.
- **Budget**: no paid APIs in the hot path.

## 2. Functional requirements

### 2.1 Ingestion
- FR-1. Fetch every configured feed every hour (at minute 51).
- FR-2. Use conditional GET (ETag / If-Modified-Since), a descriptive User-Agent, and a per-feed timeout.
- FR-3. A failing, slow or malformed feed must not abort the run. Log the failure and continue.
- FR-4. Back off on feeds that fail repeatedly. Track per-feed health (last success, consecutive failures).
- FR-5. Feeds are defined in a config file (name, URL, optional weight). Adding a feed is a config change only.
- FR-6. Deduplicate articles by normalized URL (strip tracking params, resolve redirects where cheap) and by feed GUID.
- FR-7. Strip HTML from summaries and normalize whitespace and encoding.
- FR-8. Retain articles for 30 days, then purge.

### 2.2 Clustering
- FR-9. Embed `title + summary` with a local multilingual model. Chosen: `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small` (with the `query: ` prefix), which beat `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2` on the labeled pair set (F0.5 0.93 vs 0.83).
- FR-10. Clustering is incremental. A new article is compared with the centroids of clusters active in the last 48 h by cosine similarity. At or above the threshold it joins the best cluster and the centroid is updated. Below it, a new cluster starts.
- FR-11. The threshold is tuned on a labeled set of article pairs (same story / different story), not by gut: `tests/labeled_pairs.csv` (205 pairs; `scripts/tune.py`). Current threshold for e5-small: 0.90. e5 scores are compressed (unrelated texts reach ~0.88), so thresholds are not comparable across models.
- FR-12. Guards against false merges:
  - require some overlap of named entities or rare tokens;
  - two articles from the same outlet merge only above a higher similarity threshold.
- FR-13. Clusters expire from the front page after 48 h without new articles.
- FR-14. The cluster headline is the member title closest to the centroid. The cluster image is the first available member image.

### 2.3 Front page
- FR-14b. Product shape: the page shows only the **10 most covered stories** per country (distinct outlets, recency-boosted). Single-outlet stories are not shown.
- FR-14c. Each story shows the **real headline of its representative article**, its outlet (as a link to that article), its image, and the other outlets that cover it (first four, the rest in a `<details>` toggle), each linked to its own article. Nothing is rewritten.
- FR-14d. The representative article is the **most central** one in the cluster (highest mean similarity to the others), minus a penalty for poor front-page headlines (quotes, questions, live blogs, all caps, opinion labels, very long); ties go to the earliest. Only http(s) URLs become links or images.
- FR-14e. An edition with `exclude_foreign = true` (Spain) leaves out stories about the rest of the world, with deterministic rules (`foreign.py`, no generative AI): at least 30 % of the story's articles filed under an international URL section (`/internacional/`, `/mundo/`...), or the story matches a story of another edition (similarity >= 0.91 and >= 2 shared proper-noun stems). The next stories fill the front page. Measured on labeled days with `scripts/eval_foreign.py` (tests/labeled_foreign.csv): precision 1.00, recall 1.00 on the first day (30 stories, 7 foreign); thresholds were set on that one day.
- FR-14f. An edition with `max_per_country = N` shows at most N stories about the same country (International: 1). The country of a story comes from a dictionary (`countries.toml`: country names, demonyms, capitals, cities, leaders; places weigh 1.0, people 0.5) applied to the headlines of its articles; it must hold at least half of the weight and at least 2 mentions, so stories that mix countries or mention none are never limited. Measured with `scripts/eval_countries.py` (tests/labeled_countries.csv): precision 0.92, recall 0.96 on the first labeled day (30 stories), measured on the same sample the dictionary was written from.
- FR-14g. An edition with `show_flags = true` (International) puts the flag of each story's country in its header (from the ISO code of `countries.toml`), and a globe when the story mixes countries or has no clear one. The country name is the tooltip. Spain does not show flags.
- FR-15. Output is static HTML: one frozen edition per day (see Daily editions), after ingestion and clustering.
- FR-16. Ranking: score = (number of distinct outlets in the cluster) with a recency boost. Newer last-update means a higher score. Singleton clusters rank below multi-outlet ones.
- FR-17. Each cluster shows: headline, image (if any), time of latest article, and the list of covering outlets with links to the original articles.
- FR-18. Display headline, outlet and link only. Do not republish text.
- FR-19. The page is responsive and readable without JavaScript.
- FR-20. The page is written atomically (temp file then rename), so readers never see a half-written page.

## 3. Non-functional requirements

- NFR-1. A full cycle (fetch, embed, cluster, render) finishes in under 5 minutes on the target box.
- NFR-2. RAM use stays under 2 GB, so a 4 GB box has headroom.
- NFR-3. Cycles are idempotent. Re-running on the same data changes nothing.
- NFR-4. Scheduling by cron or a systemd timer. Overlapping runs are prevented (lock file).
- NFR-5. Structured logs and a per-feed health summary (CLI command or section in the logs).
- NFR-6. Tests:
  - feed parsing against recorded fixture feeds;
  - a clustering regression test on the labeled pair set;
  - the renderer produces valid HTML.
- NFR-7. Public-readiness: serve via nginx or Caddy over HTTPS, keep the source list and attribution visible, and respect robots/ToS.

## 3b. Maintainability requirements

- MR-1. **Alerting**: notify (email or webhook) when a feed has been failing for more than N hours, or when a whole run fails or produces no page.
- MR-2. **Model versioning**: store the embedding model name and version with every embedding. Provide a `reembed` command that rebuilds all vectors and re-clusters after a model change.
- MR-3. **Clustering explainability**: a CLI command shows, for any article, its cluster, best similarity score, runner-up, and which guards fired.
- MR-4. **Schema migrations**: numbered SQL migration files applied in order, with the schema version recorded in the DB.
- MR-5. **Dependency pinning**: a lock file and a documented Python version.
- MR-6. **Reproducible deploys**: systemd units, timers and web server config are code: they live in the `personal-infra` Ansible playbook `13-agregador.yml`, which sets up a fresh box.
- MR-7. **Backups**: daily off-box copy of the SQLite file. Config, labeled pairs and fixtures live in git.
- MR-8. **Modular stages**: `fetch`, `embed`, `cluster` and `render` are separate modules with plain-data interfaces, each testable and replaceable alone.
- MR-9. **Normalization layer**: all feed-specific quirks (encodings, date formats, tracking params) are handled in one place.

## 4. Stack

Python, with `feedparser` and `httpx` for fetching, `sentence-transformers` for embeddings, `numpy` for similarity, SQLite for storage, Jinja2 for templates, and cron or a systemd timer for scheduling.

### Paywalls

Outlets whose articles often need a subscription carry `paywall = true` in `feeds.toml` and are shown with a lock. Marked: NYT, WSJ, FT, The Hindu, El País, El Mundo, ABC, Expansión. Checked with the structured data flag `isAccessibleForFree` where the sites allowed it (The Hindu, ABC, El Mundo, Expansión); NYT, WSJ, FT and El País block article downloads, so they come from public knowledge. The flag was not trusted blindly (it marks elDiario.es, which is free). Unknown: SCMP.

### Argentina (disabled; verified 2026-10-19)

## 6. Out of scope for v1

- Full-article fetching
- User accounts and personalization
- Topic categories or section pages
- Cross-language clustering
- LLM-generated summaries (optional later: judge ambiguous pairs in a grey similarity zone, or summarize clusters)
- Source bias labeling

## 7. Open questions

- Final choice of embedding model, based on speed and quality on the labeled set.
- Ranking refinements: source weights, and a penalty for outlets that publish many near-duplicate items.
- Public launch: domain, HTTPS, analytics, and how to credit sources.
